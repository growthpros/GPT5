This session's network blocked direct page fetching for substack.com subdomains and most other hosts (one test fetch to lcsd.org.uk also failed DNS). Everything comes from WebSearch result text: titles, URLs, snippets, and the search tool's summaries of them. Short quotes are included only where the search result showed the text as a snippet or quotation. They have not been checked against the live page, so treat them as "quote as shown in search results". Status labels used:
- **VERIFIED (search):** two or more consistent search results, or the author's own byline/About text shown in results.
- **UNVERIFIED:** one result only, or inferred.
- **CONFLICTING:** results disagree.

## 1. Who are these writers, and what do they actually argue about neurodivergence and spirituality?

### Takeaway
Most of the listed newsletters are real and on topic. They fall into three groups: (a) late-diagnosed autistic or ADHD writers working through evangelical or high-control religion (Mayfield, Salladin, Sellers, Matara, Originalsinfluencer); (b) believing autistic Christians writing from inside the faith (Oliver, Hardwick, Machnee, Sae, Burnett, Hopkins, Anxious Faith); (c) contemplative, mystic or alternative-spiritual voices (Kidwell, Carlson, Foldys, Jallow, Hamstead). Several names from the earlier pass turned out to be **not neurodivergent writers on faith at all**. Crystal Ball, Little Sparrow Loved, Michael F. Bird and KT Sancken are parents, allies, or unrelated to the topic. Carmen's newsletter is ADHD-focused, but no faith content surfaced.

## 3. What themes recur?

### Takeaway
Five themes recur with search evidence: God or faith as a special interest; high-control religion "fitting" and then exploiting neurodivergent brains (masking made sacred, traits blamed as sin or demons); sensory overload at church and the move to home or nature practice; rituals and silence as regulation; and menopause or burnout as the trigger for unmasking and spiritual reorientation. Justice sensitivity shows up mostly indirectly.

### Inferences
- The "late diagnosis leads to deconstruction" storyline is mostly told by US exvangelicals (Mayfield, Salladin, Originalsinfluencer) and by religious-trauma clinicians (Sellers, Matara, Piercy). ### Gaps
- No post was found that explicitly ties justice sensitivity to faith or deconversion by name.
- Sensory worship is discussed mostly as an accessibility problem, not as positive sensory spirituality (except in the pagan and nature writers).
