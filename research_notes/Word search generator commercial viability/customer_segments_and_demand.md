# Customer segments, demand signals and willingness to pay for custom themed word search puzzles (as of November 2026)

Method note for the report writer: findings come from web search result summaries gathered on 13 November 2026. I could not open most primary pages directly (Activity Connection's pricing page returned HTTP 403), so prices and statistics below are as reported in search snippets and should be re-checked on the vendor page before being quoted as exact. Items labelled "MARKETING CLAIM" come from a seller of the thing being praised. Items labelled "EVIDENCE" come from peer-reviewed research or a regulator.

## 1. Teachers and homeschoolers: usage, out-of-pocket spend, how schools buy, educational value

### Takeaway
Teachers have real personal budgets (roughly US$650 to US$1,000 a year in the US, about A$988 in Australia) and an established habit of paying under US$5 per resource or about US$6 a month for a puzzle tool, but the free competition is heavy and word searches carry a credible "busywork" reputation among pedagogy writers. No peer-reviewed study testing word searches as a learning intervention was found, for or against.

### Cited Findings
Out-of-pocket spending
- US teachers averaged US$895 of their own money in the 2024-25 school year, up 49% from US$600 in 2015 (AdoptAClassroom Spring 2025 survey, about 3,700 teachers, online questionnaire) — [AdoptAClassroom](https://www.adoptaclassroom.org/2025/06/09/2025-teacher-survey-spending-stats-classroom-needs/)
- DonorsChoose's 2025 teacher survey put the figure at US$655; the sample is teachers who use that platform — [DonorsChoose](https://blog.donorschoose.org/articles/teacher-survey-2025)
- A CouponBirds 2025 survey reported teachers expecting to spend US$1,021, up from US$915 in 2024 (coupon-site survey, methodology unclear) — [CouponBirds](https://www.couponbirds.com/research/back-to-school-expenses-2025)
- Older federal baseline: teachers spent an average US$479 in 2015-16 (flag: dated) — [Education Week](https://www.edweek.org/ew/articles/2018/05/15/the-average-teacher-spends-479-a-year.html)
- Australia: the AEU 2025 State of Our Schools survey of 10,384 public school teachers found 86.4% spend their own money on classroom supplies, averaging A$988.18 each; the union totals this at A$175 million — [AEU](https://www.aeufederal.org.au/news-media/media-releases/2025/teachers-spending-175-million-their-own-money-keep-classrooms-running-1)

What teachers pay for worksheet and puzzle tools
- WordMint (crossword and word search maker): full version US$5.99 per month with unlimited puzzle downloads plus a pre-made library — [WordMint](https://wordmint.com/handouts)
- My Worksheet Maker: unlimited free worksheets with no eventual paywall; some features reserved for paying members — [My Worksheet Maker help](https://myworksheetmaker.groovehq.com/help/how-many-worksheets-can-i-make-for-free). A Capterra listing shows US$10 with no stated billing period (unconfirmed) — [Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/10046211/My-Worksheet-Maker/)
- Puzzel.org lists a school plan at US$125 per year — [Puzzel.org pricing](https://puzzel.org/en/pricing)
- Super Teacher Worksheets lists a site licence at US$375 per year for a whole school building — [Super Teacher Worksheets](https://www.superteacherworksheets.com/site-licenses.html)
- Teachers Pay Teachers: the average resource costs less than US$5 and the average transaction is about US$15 (undated article, likely several years old) — [The 74](https://www.the74million.org/article/meet-the-etsy-of-education-online-marketplace-lets-teachers-buy-and-sell-millions-of-classroom-materials-and-lessons/)
- Free AI-assisted word search makers aimed at teachers already exist, including Canva's word search maker and Education Copilot's generator — [Canva](https://www.canva.com/word-search-maker/); [Education Copilot](https://educationcopilot.com/?p=546)

Educational value
- OPINION (influential teacher blog): Cult of Pedagogy calls word searches and word scrambles the most egregious type of "busysheet" — [Cult of Pedagogy](https://www.cultofpedagogy.com/?p=15374)
- OPINION (forum): a teacher on the Straight Dope boards describes them as useful classroom management tools "and that's about it", in a thread titled "Word search puzzles as spelling aids: it's just so wrong" — [Straight Dope](https://boards.straightdope.com/t/word-search-puzzles-as-spelling-aids-its-just-so-wrong/554745?page=3)
- MARKETING CLAIM: Twinkl, which sells printable puzzles, says word puzzles are "more than just busywork" and ideal for reinforcing spelling — [Twinkl](https://www.twinkl.com/blog/how-can-word-puzzles-help-your-child-learn)
- EVIDENCE (indirect): research links organised visual search skill to reading ability in primary school children, and one 2023 study found that training visual search on symbols can carry over to reading performance. Neither tested word search worksheets — [Scientific Reports 2023 via DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/1b143028d3d44c6bb6d43e5fc5775d25)

### Inferences
- Two price anchors exist: an individual teacher price of about US$5 to US$6 a month (WordMint) or under US$5 per resource (TPT), and a school price of US$125 to US$375 a year. A solo founder would most plausibly sell to individual teachers first; site licences mean a slower sale.
- The "busywork" critique is a positioning risk, not a demand killer: teachers who use word searches use them largely for early finishers, substitute days and classroom management. A tool that pitches "learning outcomes" invites the critique; one that pitches "fast, on-theme, low-prep" does not.
- A typed-theme generator is weakly differentiated here because teachers usually already have the word list (the week's vocabulary). The AI step saves the least effort in this segment.

### Gaps
- No survey quantifying what share of teachers use word searches or how often.
- No homeschool-specific data was found (spend, tool use, or word search use).
- No UK teacher out-of-pocket figure was found.
- No TPT price data specific to word search resources; no figure for how purchasing splits between individual and site licence.
- No peer-reviewed study directly testing word searches for vocabulary or spelling outcomes.

## 2. Senior living, memory care and activity directors

### Takeaway
This segment has clear, recurring, budgeted spend on activity content (about US$75 a year for Golden Carers, under US$200 a year implied for Activity Connection) and word searches are a standard format, with large print a recurring need. The cognitive-health evidence does not support a "prevents decline" claim for word searches: the best-known positive finding is observational, the one supportive trial tested crosswords in people with mild cognitive impairment, and the US regulator has fined a brain-game company for exactly this kind of claim.

### Cited Findings
Use and pricing
- Golden Carers: 12-month membership US$74.95 per year, no auto-renewal, 30-day guarantee; "significant discounts" for group memberships; word search is one of its listed activity formats; members can create and save their own activities — [Golden Carers](https://goldencarers.com/)
- Golden Carers members comment that they like the bigger letters on word search pages, and some describe enlarging the grid and word list themselves before printing — [Golden Carers word search page](https://goldencarers.com/word-search-occupations/6206)
- Activity Connection: monthly, 3-month, 6-month (saves more than 15% vs monthly) and 12-month plans; the annual plan is advertised as "less than 55¢ per day". MARKETING CLAIM: used as the foundation for life enrichment programming in nearly 20,000 senior living communities — [Activity Connection](https://activityconnection.com/?p=823)
- Retail benchmark for print: Rockridge Press "Extra Large-Print Easy Word Search for Seniors" lists at US$11.99 — [Bulk Bookstore](https://bulkbookstore.com/extra-large-print-easy-word-search-for-seniors-9781638074878-1). Other large-print word search books sell for about US$6 to US$13 at Walmart, including a set of four for US$12.99 — [Walmart](https://www.walmart.com/ip/3232386908)

Research on puzzles and cognition
- EVIDENCE (observational, not causal): the PROTECT online cohort (University of Exeter and King's College London) analysed 19,078 cognitively healthy adults aged 50 to 93 by self-reported word puzzle frequency. All 14 cognitive measures showed significant effects of frequency; those who never did puzzles scored lowest. The authors state that future work must determine whether puzzles can influence cognitive trajectory. Published in the International Journal of Geriatric Psychiatry, 2018/2019 (flag: dated, cross-sectional) — [University of Exeter repository](https://ore.exeter.ac.uk/repository/handle/10871/39482)
- Press coverage of PROTECT reported brain function "equivalent to ten years younger" for regular puzzlers; this is a press-release framing of a correlation — [ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2019/05/190516082349.htm)
- EVIDENCE (small randomised trial, crosswords not word searches): Devanand et al., NEJM Evidence, October 2022. 107 participants with mild cognitive impairment did web-based crosswords or web-based cognitive games for 12 weeks plus boosters, followed to 78 weeks. Crosswords did better on ADAS-Cog (difference -1.44, 95% CI -2.83 to -0.06, P=0.04), on daily functioning, and showed less brain shrinkage on MRI. The authors call for replication with a larger trial and an inactive control group — [Columbia Psychiatry](https://www.columbiapsychiatry.org/node/25213)
- EVIDENCE (systematic review): the Cochrane review of 12 or more weeks of computerised cognitive training in cognitively healthy older people found low-quality evidence of a slight improvement in global cognition at the end of training and no clear evidence of an effect persisting at 12 months (2020 update; the 2019 version found "little evidence") — [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32104914/); [PMC, 2019 version](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6414816)
- REGULATORY: in January 2016 Lumos Labs (Lumosity) agreed to pay US$2 million to settle FTC charges that it claimed, without adequate evidence, that its games delay age-related decline and protect against mild cognitive impairment and dementia. The order requires competent and reliable scientific evidence for future benefit claims — [FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/node/44800)

### Inferences
- "Less than 55¢ per day" implies an Activity Connection annual price below roughly US$200. Combined with Golden Carers at US$74.95, the going rate for a full activity-content library is roughly US$75 to US$200 per community per year. A single-format word search tool would have to price well below that or sell on speed and customisation (resident names, local places, this month's theme).
- Custom themes are a real fit: activity calendars are theme-driven (holidays, decades, reminiscence topics), and members already hand-adjust size before printing. Large print, low grid density, no diagonal or backwards words, and one-click printing matter more here than on-screen play.
- Marketing must say "enjoyable, engaging activity", not "improves memory" or "prevents dementia". The evidence is correlational for word puzzles in general, absent for word searches specifically, and the Lumosity case shows the legal exposure in the US.
- Buyers are staff with small discretionary budgets or a corporate subscription; the purchase may need a manager's sign-off, though the price point is low enough to expense.

### Gaps
- Exact Activity Connection dollar prices could not be retrieved (page blocked).
- No pricing found for S&S Worldwide or ElderSong activity content.
- No UK or Australian vendor pricing beyond Golden Carers (Australian-founded, priced in USD).
- No study was found that tests word searches (as distinct from crosswords) for cognitive outcomes in older adults. The ACTIVE trial was not checked in this pass.
- No data on how often word searches appear on activity calendars.

## 3. Neurodivergent users (ADHD, autism, dyslexia) and accessibility

### Takeaway
There is no peer-reviewed evidence that word searches help ADHD or autistic people with focus or regulation; what exists is vendor copy, a handful of low-priced themed puzzle books, and plausible but untested ideas about low-demand activity and special-interest themes. The opportunity is an accessibility-first product in a field with no visible leader, sold on comfort and control, not on therapeutic benefit.

### Cited Findings
- No peer-reviewed study on word searches for ADHD or autism was returned by search; results were sellers, puzzle sites and blogs — [search results including Bits and Pieces](https://www.bitsandpieces.com/blogs/blog-post/best-puzzles-for-adhd)
- MARKETING CLAIM: a puzzle retailer's article notes some puzzle styles may feel more frustrating than fun for people with ADHD, so format matters — [Bits and Pieces](https://www.bitsandpieces.com/blogs/blog-post/best-puzzles-for-adhd)
- MARKETING CLAIM / vendor opinion: a game studio argues ADHD is about how focus is regulated and that puzzles with quick feedback suit that — [Inkwell Games](https://blog.inkwellgames.com/posts/logical-puzzles-and-adhd)
- Existing products: an "ADHD Word Search Book" (2025, 50+ themed puzzles, 6x9 format) is sold as a device-free way to settle the mind for breaks, travel or therapy sessions — [Lulu](https://www.lulu.com/shop/dk-puzzle-books/adhd-word-search-book/paperback/product-7kvzd6w.html). An "Anxiety Relief Word Search" large-print series (24 puzzles per volume) targets adults with chronic illness — [Spoonie Magic](https://reach.dog/shop/spoonie-magic/products/anxiety-relief-word-search)
- Most "ADHD word search" content online is psychoeducation vocabulary puzzles (words such as hyperactivity, inattentive, medication) made by teachers on WordMint and TES, not puzzles designed for neurodivergent solvers — [WordMint](https://wordmint.com/public_puzzles/65321); [TES](https://tes.com/teaching-resource/national-adhd-awarness-word-search-puzzle-worksheet-activities-13113549)
- A children's mental health resource site lists word searches among mentally stimulating activities for children with ADHD (seller of worksheets) — [Mental Health Center Kids](https://mentalhealthcenterkids.com/blogs/articles/adhd-games-and-activities-for-kids)

Accessibility guidance (British Dyslexia Association style guide, as described by secondary sources; 2018 and 2023 versions)
- Use sans-serif fonts such as Arial, Verdana or Tahoma; 12 to 14 point as the ideal body size, larger for some readers; headings at least 20% larger — [Wigan Council copy of the BDA guide](https://www.wigan.gov.uk/Docs/PDF/Business/Professionals/SEN/HEFA/Dyslexia-Style-Guide.pdf)
- Dark text on a light but not white background (cream or soft pastel); single-colour backgrounds; avoid green and red/pink — [Wigan Council copy of the BDA guide](https://www.wigan.gov.uk/Docs/PDF/Business/Professionals/SEN/HEFA/Dyslexia-Style-Guide.pdf)
- Larger inter-letter spacing, around 35% of average letter width (figure seen in one secondary article quoting the 2018 guide) — [Smashing Magazine](https://smashingmagazine.com/2021/11/dyslexia-friendly-mode-website)
- Dyslexia Scotland (separate body) recommends about 1.5 line spacing — [Dyslexia Scotland](https://dyslexiascotland.org.uk/dyslexia-friendly-typed-formats)

### Inferences
- The founder's niche gives distribution and credibility, not proven demand. Nothing found shows neurodivergent adults searching for or paying a premium for word searches; the visible products are US$6 to US$12 books.
- The strongest honest pitch is control over the experience: any special interest as a theme, no timer, no streaks, no ads or animation, adjustable grid size and letter spacing, cream background, large print, optional no-diagonals mode, pause and resume. These follow from the dyslexia guidance and from the "format matters" point, and most free generators are ad-heavy.
- Word searches are a hard case for dyslexic users (dense letter grids, reversed and diagonal strings). Settings that restrict direction and density are a requirement for this audience, and a claim of being "dyslexia friendly" needs testing with users.
- Screen reader support for a letter grid is technically difficult and no existing product was found that solves it; it should not be promised without building and testing it.
- Any health-style claim (focus, regulation, calming) has no evidence base and carries the same risk as the brain-training claims in section 2.

### Gaps
- No community discussion (Reddit, ADHD or autism forums) was retrieved in this pass; the "low-demand activity" and "special interest themes" angles are unverified hypotheses.
- No WCAG-specific findings were gathered; the BDA guide itself was not read directly.
- No data on the size of the neurodivergent adult market for puzzles or their willingness to pay.
- No evidence found on timers or visual clutter for ADHD or autistic solvers specifically.

## 4. Other buyers: events, therapists and SLPs, ESL, churches, marketers, employee engagement, publishers

### Takeaway
Event hosts are the clearest one-off payers (about US$3 to US$5 for a personalised printable), while the only data point found for branded puzzles as lead magnets is negative. Therapist use is plausible but backed only by vendor blogs, and publishers are served by an established B2B platform.

### Cited Findings
- Events: Etsy personalised word search printables for weddings, baby showers and bridal showers show sale prices around US$4.00 to US$4.75 (list prices US$15.99 to US$18.99, almost always discounted); themed baby shower word searches around US$3 to US$5 — [Etsy listing](https://www.etsy.com/listing/4428251159); [Etsy listing](https://www.etsy.com/listing/1332219508/)
- Events: a custom wedding "sip and search" word search card on Zazzle is about US$3.75 per card; a bridal shower word search with answer key on Gumroad is US$4.10 — [Zazzle](https://www.zazzle.com.au/sip_and_search_custom_word_wedding_search_puzzle_invitation-256556647192298026); [Gumroad](https://sophieadams.gumroad.com/l/bridal-word-search)
- Events: Fiverr sellers offer custom word search creation as a service — [Fiverr](https://www.fiverr.com/daniellelynn55/create-custom-word-search-puzzles-for-you)
- Speech therapy: MARKETING CLAIM from a speech app's blog suggests building a custom puzzle around a target sound and having the child say each word aloud when found; no controlled study cited — [Speech Blubs](https://speechblubs.com/blog/custom-word-search-for-kids-speech-learning-fun)
- Marketers: one marketer's self-run test reported a word search lead magnet converting at 9% against 61% for a plain checklist covering the same content (single anecdotal test) — [Lilach Bullock](https://www.lilachbullock.com/create-printable-word-search-puzzles-for-customers/)
- Publishers and brands: Amuse Labs' PuzzleMe platform offers customisable branded games including word search, AI-assisted creation for all users, a WordPress plugin and a Microsoft marketplace listing; branded campaign puzzles can take up to 4 weeks lead time for a new client — [Amuse Labs FAQ](https://amuselabs.com/faqs/); [Amuse Labs pricing page](https://amuselabs.com/pricing)
- Self-publishers (Amazon KDP puzzle books) pay for generators: Puzzle Maker Pro starts at about US$47 per module, one-time — [Univers Studio comparison](https://univers.studio/compare/univers-studio-vs-puzzle-maker-pro/). A Gumroad word search book generator is listed at £34.99 a year and another creator tool at US$15 a month — [KDP Ready on Gumroad](https://kdpready.gumroad.com/l/advanced-word-search-pbg); [Gumroad](https://marinaartdesign.gumroad.com/l/cvufs)

### Inferences
- Event hosts pay for a finished, pretty, personalised printable, not for a generator. A typed-theme tool competes here only if the output looks as good as an Etsy template; the purchase is one-off and seasonal by life event.
- KDP self-publishers show the highest tool prices found (US$47 one-time, £35 a year, US$15 a month) because the puzzle is their inventory. They need bulk export and commercial rights, and this market is crowded with low-quality books.
- Branded lead magnets look weak on the single data point available; treat as unproven.

### Gaps
- No data found for churches, ESL teachers, employee engagement or team building, or newsletter publishers' willingness to pay.
- Amuse Labs' actual prices were not retrieved.
- No occupational therapy evidence found; SLP use rests on vendor blogs.
- Etsy sales volumes for custom word searches were not found.

## 5. Demand size signals: search volume, trends, market size, seasonality

### Takeaway
Reliable keyword volumes for "word search maker", "word search generator", "custom word search" and "AI word search generator" could not be obtained from public sources; the only figure found is for the head term "word search" and it comes from a tool with internal inconsistencies. The report writer should treat demand size as unverified and recommend the founder pull exact figures from Google Keyword Planner or Ahrefs before deciding.

### Cited Findings
- RankHero reports "word search" at 823,000 average monthly searches, 55% from the US, keyword difficulty 28/100, with a monthly table showing 1,000,000 in October and November and 823,000 in December and January. CAUTION: the page's dates are internally inconsistent, a sister page gives difficulty 16/100 for "wordsearch", and the tool appears to be oriented to Etsy sellers — [RankHero](https://www.rankhero.com/keywords/word-search)
- RankHero reports "word puzzles" at 110,000 monthly searches, difficulty 26/100 (same caution) — [RankHero](https://www.rankhero.com/keywords/word-puzzles)
- Keyword tools disagree widely with each other and report 12-month averages that hide seasonal peaks — [Practical Ecommerce](https://www.practicalecommerce.com/keyword-volume-google-vs-semrush-vs-ahrefs)
- Supply-side signal: AI word search generators already exist from Canva, Pixa, CapCut's Dreamina, SheetWorks, Education Copilot and WordSearch.diy (launched on Product Hunt) — [Canva](https://www.canva.com/word-search-maker/); [Pixa](https://www.pixa.com/create/word-search-generator); [Product Hunt](https://www.producthunt.com/products/wordsearch-diy/launches); [SheetWorks](https://sheetworks.ai/tools/word-search)
- Adjacent market, digital word games: NYT Games says players solved more than 11 billion puzzles on its platform in 2025 — [PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/nyt-games-multiplayer-word-game-crossplay-surpasses-one-million-downloads)
- Words With Friends generated an estimated US$43 million gross revenue in 2025 (AppMagic estimate) — [PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/nyt-games-multiplayer-word-game-crossplay-surpasses-one-million-downloads)
- Mobile puzzle games overall: over 9.7 billion downloads and US$10 billion in in-app purchase revenue in 2025 (one analyst source; scope is all puzzle sub-genres, dominated by match-3 style games) — [Naavik](https://naavik.co/digest/how-niche-subgenres-are-reshaping-the-mobile-puzzle-market/)

### Inferences
- The RankHero monthly table, if taken at face value, shows a higher October to November level than December to January, which would fit a back-to-school and autumn holiday peak. This is weak evidence from an unreliable source.
- The presence of Canva and several AI-native entrants shows the "AI word search from a theme" idea is already commoditised as a free feature. Search demand almost certainly exists, but ranking for generic terms against Canva and long-established free generators is unlikely for a new solo site.
- Mobile game revenue figures say little about willingness to pay for a custom puzzle maker; they describe ad and in-app revenue from mass-market play.

### Gaps
- No volume found for "word search maker", "word search generator", "custom word search", "AI word search generator" or "printable word search".
- No Google Trends direction was obtained.
- No reliable market size for brain-training apps, printable worksheets or the print puzzle book market; results were low-quality market-report spam and were excluded.
- No traffic estimates for incumbents (for example thewordsearch.com, Discovery Puzzlemaker).
- No seasonality data beyond the weak inference above.

### Inferences
- A standalone word search generator supports roughly US$5 to US$6 a month or US$30 to US$50 a year from an individual. At those prices a solo founder needs on the order of thousands of subscribers for a meaningful income, so the product looks more viable as a lead generator or credibility piece for the consulting business than as a profit centre.
- Segment ranking on the evidence gathered (my judgement, for the writer to weigh):
  1. Senior living and memory care activity staff: budgeted recurring spend, theme-driven calendars, a documented large-print need, and customisation that members currently do by hand. Weakness: incumbents bundle word searches into a full library at US$75 a year.
  2. Neurodivergent and accessibility-first users: best fit with the founder's audience and no visible leader, but demand and willingness to pay are unproven. Best treated as the product's design stance across segments.
  3. Teachers: largest and most reachable audience with known price anchors, but saturated with free tools and exposed to the busywork critique.
  4. Event hosts: proven one-off payments, but they buy design, not generation.
  5. Self-publishers: highest tool prices, but a crowded market and misaligned with the founder's brand.
  6. Marketers and lead magnets: the one data point is negative.
- A combined position is available: an accessibility-first generator (large print, cream background, no timers, direction controls) serves memory care, neurodivergent users and special education teachers with one feature set.

### Gaps
- No direct willingness-to-pay research (surveys or price tests) exists for any segment; all figures are observed list prices of adjacent products.
- No conversion or churn data for any word search or worksheet subscription.
- No UK or Australian price points apart from the AEU spending figure and Golden Carers.
- Activity Connection's exact tier prices and Amuse Labs' plan prices were not retrieved.
